Match observations:

Agricultural Hall
Allentown, PA
July 19th, 1991
Attendance: 900
Average Ticket: $12.00
Gate: $10,800

TWA's second show, and its first outside Philadelphia, drew 900 to the
old WWF television building at the Allentown Fairgrounds. That's about 
60 percent of the hall and a little under the debut at McGonigle Hall, 
which isn't bad for a Friday spot show with no television and two of the 
bigger names out of the country. Bam Bam Bigelow is in Japan for New Japan's 
summer tour and Billy Joe Travis is with All Japan, and neither is expected 
back until the end of the month.

The bad news of the night came in the second match. The Nightstalker tore 
his left biceps in a win over Bunkhouse Buck and is expected to miss 
the rest of the summer. That's a real loss for this group. 
He had the best match at the Philadelphia debut, he was the closest thing 
TWA has to a homegrown star, and he was the favorite going into the title
tournament that starts in Trenton next week. Word is the office was
planning to build around him, so the tournament has been rebooked without
him.

Austin Idol opened the show with a promo saying Bigelow ran to Japan to
get out of their rematch. Bob Armstrong answered with Buddy Landell, and Idol, 
who is also the booker, accepted a match for later in the night. It got the 
crowd going and did what it needed to do, which was keep the Idol and Bigelow 
program alive with Bigelow out of the country.

Chris Candido beat J.T. Smith in the opener and it was the best match on 
the show. Candido is 2-0 in TWA and looks like he's being set up for something
bigger. Smith made him look great.

The Nightstalker pinned Buck with the Meltdown. The match was short and mostly
Nightstalker, and it wasn't clear to anyone in the building that he was 
hurt until after.

The Bruise Brothers beat Bad News Brown and Mr. Sandman when Ron Harris pinned
Sandman with a big boot. This was the worst thing on the show and the Harrises'
second bad match in two TWA appearances. They're being pushed as monsters in 
WCW, and they need short matches against people who can carry them.

Idol pinned Armstrong after a waistlock takedown in a solid veteran match,
but replays showed Idol had both feet on the ropes for the pin. The
referee never saw it and counted three. Buddy Landell, in Armstrong's
corner, was in the referee's face about it after the bell, but the
decision stands. It's exactly what you'd expect from a heel who opened the
show bragging, and it gives Landell and Armstrong a reason to want Idol
again.  

Al Snow pinned Action Jackson with the Snow Plow in the main event, which ran 
almost twenty minutes and finally settled their draw from Philadelphia. Good 
match, though it felt long for the second half. Afterward they shook hands and 
both said they'd see each other again in the tournament. The crowd stood for it.

Card rating of **1/4 is a small improvement over the debut. The Nightstalker
injury is the story here, and TWA will have to get through the summer with 
Bigelow still overseas, the Bruise Brothers struggling, and its best young star 
on the shelf. The eight-man tournament for the first TWA Heavyweight Title 
starts at the CYO Center in Trenton on July 27th.
