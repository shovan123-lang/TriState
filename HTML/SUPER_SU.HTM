Match observations:

McGonigle Hall
Philadelphia, PA
June 29th, 1991
Attendance: 1,180 (about 1,050 paid)
Average Ticket: $12.00
Gate: $12,600

The newest independent in the Northeast opened its doors at Temple University's
McGonigle Hall. That isn't big business by WWF or WCW standards, and the building
was about a third full, but for a debut show with no television in a city where 
the WWF runs the Spectrum every month, it's a respectable number. 
The crowd was loud, knew who everyone was, and stayed hot for most of the night.

The promoter is Joel Goodhart, a familiar name to Philadelphia fans, and the 
money behind the group is said to include local businessman Tod Gordon.
The booker is Austin Idol, who also booked himself into the main event 
and walked out of it without losing, which is about as old-school as it gets.
Goodhart says the plan is spot shows around Pennsylvania, New Jersey and 
Delaware, with Allentown and Trenton dates in July and McGonigle Hall as 
the monthly home base.

The roster is a mix of names fans know from national television, like Bam Bam
Bigelow, Bad News Brown, Austin Idol, Buddy Landell and Bob Armstrong, 
and younger independent talent like Al Snow, Chris Candido, Mr. Sandman, 
J.T. Smith and The Nightstalker. The problem is keeping it together. Bigelow
leaves next week for New Japan's summer tour, Billy Joe Travis is headed to 
All Japan, and the Bruise Brothers start with WCW at the Philadelphia Civic 
Center next weekend. 

The Harrises are said to have told the office they'll work both when dates
allow.

Snow and Jackson opened with a twenty-minute draw. Snow is a good worker 
and carried most of it, but opening a debut show with a draw is a booking
choice I'd question. The crowd was into the first half and sat down for the
second.

The Nightstalker and J.T. Smith had the best match on the show. 
The Nightstalker is a big man who moves well for his size, and Smith 
took a beating and bumped all over the building to make him look like 
a monster. If this group has a star of its own, it might be him.

Landell and Armstrong got the crowd behind them, but the tag dragged badly 
in the middle. Having the Harrises tap out a week before their WCW debut is 
a strange call.

Bad News Brown over Bunkhouse Buck was too short to be anything. Brown got a 
nice reaction coming out, which says the name still means something here.

Candido is 19 and already one of the better young workers on the East Coast
independent scene. He and Travis didn't have the time to do much, but what 
they did was clean.

In the main event, Bigelow was the best thing in it and the crowd wanted to
see him win. Idol, as booker, put himself in a draw with the guy leaving for
Japan, so the rematch can't happen until at least August.

Two time-limit draws on a six-match card is too many, and the undercard 
needed more time where it counted and less where it didn't. But the building,
the crowd and the roster are good enough that this group could stick around if 
it can hold on to its talent through the summer.
